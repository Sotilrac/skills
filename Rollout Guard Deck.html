Positioning statement

For skeptical platform teams on K8s.

Rollout Guard catches bad deploys before your on-call gets paged, using telemetry your team already emits.
Who it's for
Platform Engineering Managers running canary and staged rollouts on Kubernetes, already skeptical of automated deploy-safety tools and tired of maintaining homegrown scripts.
What it does
Before your on-call sees the page, Rollout Guard stops bad canaries in seconds and pauses staged rollouts before the next cohort promotes — reading the telemetry your team already emits.
Why it's different
It plugs into Argo Rollouts, Spinnaker, and GitHub Actions without replacing them. Every halt is a record your SRE can read and tune. Useful on day one — not after a quarter of config.

market pattern Teams shipping their own canary analysis typically spend 3–6 months on custom work before they trust it overnight. Rollout Guard fills this opportunity.
assumption   Kubernetes-first v1.

Value propositions

What a Platform Manager actually cares about.

01
Stop bad deploys before your on-call gets the page
A canary stage is only safe if something is watching closely enough to react. Rollout Guard reads your existing metrics, detects the regression, and halts canaries in seconds — before an engineer has acknowledged the alert. On staged rollouts, it pauses the promotion before the next cohort, so the blast radius stops widening.
do the math A canary held at 5% for 2 minutes exposes roughly 100× fewer users than one at 50% for 10.
02
Keep the deploy tools you already standardized on
Argo Rollouts stays. Spinnaker stays. GitHub Actions stays. Rollout Guard adds the safety signal those tools always needed through APIs they already expose. No migration. No new CD platform.
03
Hand your SRE a record they can paste into a postmortem
When Rollout Guard stops a rollout, canary stage or staged cohort, your team reads exactly why: which signal, by how much, against which baseline. Not a black-box model that hands you a confidence score.
market pattern Most deploy-safety tools produce a confidence score. Few produce a receipt.

Objection handling

Things a skeptical PM says out loud.

“
It's going to cry wolf and we'll turn it off in a week.
False halts are why most automated gates get disabled inside a month. Rollout Guard uses sequential statistical testing that fires on real regression, not canary noise. Stage-aware: a spike at 5% is evaluated differently than at 50%. Every halt is readable — audit the call and tune thresholds in plain YAML.
We'd rather you leave it on in prod than love the demo.
“
We can do this with Prometheus and Argo Analysis Templates for free.
Should your platform engineers be writing and maintaining those queries, or shipping next quarter's roadmap? The DIY ceiling arrives fast. Rollout Guard is the opinionated, maintained version of what a strong platform team eventually builds, without the quarters of tuning or the bus-factor problem.
Analysis Templates and Kayenta are real options. They're also the reason this product exists.

Process

How I worked.

Approach

Buyer empathy first. Everything else flows from there.
I started with market & category and a buyer empathy exercise to lock the ICP before crafting the messaging. Labeling product assumptions grounded me in what Rollout Guard does and why a Platform lead would care. From there: positioning, value props & messaging, objections, and the launch artifact reflected back the buyer's pain points.

AI use — and overrides

Claude as a thinking partner on structure and variants.
Product claims — AI conflated canary and staged rollouts, kept them distinct to lead with technical credibility.
Voice & messaging — AI first drafts read like every other ENT DevOps landing page or verbatim product features, refined with a buyer empathy exercise and pain points.
Pressure testing — Used AI to simulate ICP buyer to brainstorm and solidify objection-handling.

What I'd validate first

Confirm product metrics & customer language.
Include speed-to-halt (high) + false-positive rate (low) metrics on messaging to lead with impact. Incorporate real customer language into the launch artifact and potentially include a visionary quote from an excited beta user.
